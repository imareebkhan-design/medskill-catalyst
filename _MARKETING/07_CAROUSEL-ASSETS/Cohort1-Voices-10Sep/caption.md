## LinkedIn (same slides, longer runway)

We asked our first cohort a single question: what do you understand about MedTech now that you did not before you joined?

Their answers are on the slides, unedited.

What strikes me is that none of them describe learning a syllabus. They describe a change in what they can see.

Simarpreet arrived with the common assumption — that MedTech careers mean working on devices. Coronary stents, specifically. He now talks about clinical applications specialists and supply chain as live options.

Gursheen came in already fluent in risk classification and regulatory pathways. The gap was commercial: how hospitals and healthcare organisations actually purchase, and how compliance connects to getting a product to market.

Khushi's answer is the one I'd put in front of anyone considering this field. She describes MedTech as a collaborative ecosystem — patients, doctors, biomedical engineers, hospitals, manufacturers, distributors, regulators — where each stakeholder shapes whether a technology is adopted and improved.

That is the shift we are trying to produce. Not a certificate. A working model of an industry, and an honest view of where you might fit in it.

Nobody in Cohort 1 has finished yet. That is rather the point — this is a founding cohort, and we are publishing what they say while they are still in it.

Cohort 2 starts 26 September. A discovery call is the way to find out whether it is a fit. It genuinely isn't for everyone.
