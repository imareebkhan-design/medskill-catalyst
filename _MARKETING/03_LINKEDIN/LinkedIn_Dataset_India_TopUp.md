### Post IN.4 — Arun Kumar Sharma · *Three books for a long pharma career* (started as an MR)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7419584014534983681/

```
Three books that helped me stay the course in a long pharma career

When I started my career as a Medical Representative, I thought growth would be quick if I worked hard enough.
Reality taught me something else.
In pharma, progress is often slow. Some months go exactly as planned. Many don't. You face rejection, pressure, missed numbers, sudden market shifts—and still you show up the next morning and do the work again.
Over the years, a few books helped me stay steady when outcomes were taking time. Not motivational in the "feel good" sense—more like practical reminders when the journey feels long.
1) Grit — Angela Duckworth

 This book put language to what most long-career professionals eventually learn: talent helps, but it's perseverance that carries you through the dull, difficult stretches.
 "Grit is passion and perseverance for very long-term goals."

2) The Hard Thing About Hard Things — Ben Horowitz

 As responsibilities grow, leadership becomes less about choosing between right and wrong, and more about choosing between two hard options. This book is honest about that reality—and that honesty helps.
 "There are no shortcuts to knowledge, especially knowledge gained from personal experience."

3) Atomic Habits — James Clear

 In a long career, intensity comes and goes. Systems stay. Small, repeated actions—calls, follow-ups, preparation, discipline—quietly compound. This book helped me focus on consistency over bursts of effort.
 "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."
If you've had a long run-in sales, marketing, or leadership—what's one book that genuinely stayed with you (and why)?

#Pharma #Leadership #Sales #CareerJourney #Learning #Books #Grit #AtomicHabits

### Post IN.10 — Arun Kumar Sharma · *Win–win field operating rhythm* (field-ops leadership)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7374438292286574592/

```
We all want the same thing: stronger customer conversations, clean execution, faster decisions.

 On the ground, though, fixed customer slots + travel + ad-hoc check-ins can collide—and everyone feels it: the field, line managers, and HO.
A win–win operating rhythm (14-day pilot):
1) Protect Customer Hours
 Pre-agreed visit blocks with no internal calls. Managers get a clear window for follow-ups outside these hours.
2) One Smart Huddle
 12–15 minutes at a fixed time (mid-day or day-end). Predictable for HO; calming for the field.
3) Go Lightweight Async
 90-second voice note or 3-tap form: outcome, next action, date. Managers get cleaner signals, fewer chase calls.
4) Route-Aware Planning
 Plans that respect travel and fixed appointment windows. Fewer drops, more meaningful time with customers.
5) Measure Impact, Not Just Motion
 Track conversion, repeat orders, liquidation velocity, collection days alongside call counts. Better coaching data for managers, better focus for teams.
Why managers & HO will like this:
Fewer interruptions to orchestrate
Cleaner, comparable data at day's end
A cadence that's easier to coach and scale
Pilot this in one territory for two weeks. Watch appointment adherence, order value per visit, and collection velocity. If the signal improves, scale it.
When we protect field time, we protect the plan.
 Partnership over pressure—that's how execution gets easier for everyone.
