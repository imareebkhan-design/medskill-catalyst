## POST

Two people with the same pharma background applied for the same device role. One got a call. One didn't.

The difference wasn't the experience. It was how they wrote it.

Here's roughly what the first CV said:

"Promoted products to doctors. Achieved 110% of sales target. Managed a territory of 150 doctors."

All true. All in pharma language. When that lands on a hiring manager's desk, they have to stop and work out how it maps to a device role, and with forty other CVs open, most won't do that for you.

Here's the same experience, written for the device world:

"Built and managed clinical relationships across a 150-account territory. Drove adoption of a specialised product against target. Comfortable operating in the clinical settings where the product is used."

Nothing invented. Same person, same work. It's just written in the language the reader is actually scanning for.

That gap, between what you've done and how you describe it, is the most common reason good pharma people get filtered out before anyone really reads them. And almost nobody teaches how to close it.
