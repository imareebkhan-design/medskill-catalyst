You've been doing the hardest part of medical device sales for years. You just did it in pharma.

We asked which of the four skills was already yours. Territory and KOL management won, by a clear margin.

In device sales, that skill isn't a nice-to-have. It's the job.

So here's the role, decoded.

What it actually is

You sell devices, implants and capital equipment to hospitals and surgeons. Some days you're in the operating room while your product is in use. The territory you've worked for years and the surgeons who trust you aren't background. They're the product.

Who hires for it

Medtronic. Abbott. Boston Scientific. Stryker. Siemens Healthineers. B. Braun. And a long list of mid-size firms you've never heard of that pay well and promote faster.

The day to day

Fewer calls than pharma, deeper ones. Longer cycles. A smaller set of clinicians you actually know by name. More time in hospitals, less time chasing a daily number.

Honest difficulty: medium

Your pharma background is respected. But you'll sit across from people who already have device experience. The ones who get in prove they understand the clinical setting and how a hospital actually buys, not just that they can sell.

The salary question

Everyone asks it. There's a real range, and it usually starts above where pharma sales tops out. We put the actual numbers on screen in the masterclass, not in a caption.
