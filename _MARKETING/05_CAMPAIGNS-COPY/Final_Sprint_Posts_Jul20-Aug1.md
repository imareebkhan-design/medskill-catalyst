## Tue 21 Jul - CV translation, line by line · Gagan · Medium

Same person. Same experience. One version of the line gets ignored, the other gets a callback.

Version one, the way most MRs write it: "Consistently achieved 100%+ of sales target for 4 consecutive years."

Version two: "Grew prescription share for a cardiac brand across 60 doctors in Pune, including 12 interventional cardiologists, by running therapy-awareness sessions in cath labs."

The first line tells me you hit a number. Every CV on my desk hits a number. The second line tells me you know your way around a cath lab, you can talk to an interventional cardiologist without being walked out, and you understand the therapy, not just the target. That is a MedTech clinical specialist profile hiding inside a pharma CV.

I spent 25 years reading CVs at companies like Pfizer and Medtronic. The pharma people who got MedTech interviews were almost never more qualified than the ones who did not. They had translated their experience into the language the filter reads.

In the cohort we do this with your actual CV, line by line, until a MedTech hiring manager can see you in it.

Take one line from your own CV. Does it say what you did, or just what you achieved? 👇

## Wed 22 Jul - The 60-second scan · Gagan · Medium

When your CV opened on my screen, you had about 60 seconds. Here is exactly where those seconds went.

Seconds 1 to 10: current company and role. I am placing you in the market. Pharma field sales, pharma marketing, QA, fresh graduate.

Seconds 10 to 25: therapy area and customers. Cardiology? Ortho? Who did you actually sit across from, GPs or surgeons? This decides which of our openings you could even map to.

Seconds 25 to 45: proof of ownership. Not the achievement wall. One or two lines where you clearly ran something: a territory, a launch, a doctor relationship that moved.

Seconds 45 to 60: the gaps. Job hops without a story. Buzzwords where specifics should be. An objective statement telling me you are a "dynamic team player seeking a challenging role." That line has never once helped a candidate.

Notice what I never got to: your college, your percentage, your hobbies.

Most pharma CVs fail this scan not because the person is weak but because the CV spends its 60 seconds on the wrong things. That is fixable. It is exactly the kind of thing we fix in the cohort, before the applications go out.

Be honest: where do the first 10 lines of your CV spend my 60 seconds? 👇

## Tue 28 Jul - "Is it too late for me?" · Gagan · Hard

"Sir, I am 34. Honestly, is it too late?"

I have been asked a version of this four times this week, so let me answer it once, properly.

In 25 years of hiring for MedTech, I never once rejected a candidate for being 32 instead of 26. I rejected people whose experience did not map to the role, and I hired people whose experience did. Age was never the variable. Translation was.

A 34-year-old MR with eight years of cardiology relationships is not behind. For a clinical specialist or key account role, that person is often ahead of a 26-year-old with an MBA and no operating theatre hours. The problem is that their CV usually says "achieved targets" instead of saying any of that.

What is true: the move gets harder the longer your experience stays untranslated, because every extra year in the same role makes the same story longer, not stronger. The market is growing around you either way. India's medical device industry is projected to reach $50B by 2030 {{VERIFY: IBEF}}. The roles will exist. The question is whether your CV will be legible to them.

So no, 34 is not too late. But "I will think about it next year" has a real cost, and I have watched people pay it for two decades.
