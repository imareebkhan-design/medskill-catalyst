If you can sell on outcomes instead of features, there's a role where that's the entire job. And it sits closer to the top of the company.

The last skill you voted on was solution selling. Selling on outcomes and total cost, not a feature list.

Medical affairs and market access.

What it actually is

You build and carry the value story for a device. Why it works, what it saves a hospital, the evidence behind it, and how it gets funded and adopted. You work with KOLs, hospitals and payers, not to push a product, but to prove its case.

Who hires for it

Larger device and diagnostics companies. Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific, Siemens Healthineers, Stryker, and most firms scaling a product that needs an economic and clinical argument behind it.

The day to day

Evidence, health economics, KOL relationships, and stakeholder conversations. Less daily selling, more strategy. The same instinct you used to win a doctor's trust now gets used to win an institution's.

Honest difficulty: medium to hard

It usually rewards some experience, strong communication and comfort with data. For a pharma professional who's tired of the daily number and wants to work on the bigger argument, it's one of the most natural moves on this list.

The salary question

This is one of the higher-paying tracks in MedTech, and the range reflects it. The actual figures go up in the masterclass.
