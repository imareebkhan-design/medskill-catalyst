## 1. LINKEDIN JOURNEY ARCS

### Steve Adamson — "MedTech sales advocate"

**Background:** 20+ years in medical device sales and sales management. Notably, he **left high-school head coaching and teaching in 2011 to break into the device world** — then rose through rep → branch/corporate accounts manager roles, including managing Stryker Spine contracts and pricing across 15 Midwestern states and national IDN/GPO accounts (Trinity Health, CHI, HCA, Intermountain) plus VA/DOD government contracts.
**Positioning:** His LinkedIn headline frames him as a *"Problem solver | MedTech sales advocate."* The word that matters is **advocate** — he positions as a champion of the field and of people trying to enter it, not as a hard-sell coach with a funnel.
**Content role:** Practitioner POV and encouragement, not productized course pushing. This is a **tone model**, not a tactics model.

**Journey arc pattern:** Teacher/coach → device rep → sales leadership → "advocate" voice (credibility from doing the job, not from selling a course about it)

**Why he matters to MedSkills:** He is living proof of the *exact* career-switch story your ICPs need — a non-sales professional (teacher) who crossed into device sales and built a 20-year career. His "advocate, not salesman" framing is the single most on-brand tone in this batch for your "earn trust, don't pitch" mandate. The teacher→device arc is structurally identical to your MR→MedTech and B.Pharm→regulatory arcs.

### MedTech Blueprint / Break Into MedDevice (breakintomeddevice.com)

**Positioning:** *"How to Break Into Medical Device Sales"* — an **insider's toolkit built by an active rep** (someone still in the field, not a full-time coach). The credibility hook is "I'm currently doing the job I'm teaching you to get."
**Product architecture (this is the interesting part):** They productize the *break-in itself like a sales territory.* Tiered offers:

- **The Playbook** — "treat your break-in like a revenue territory": interview scripts, resume templates, salary-negotiation frameworks, a 6-phase entry roadmap.
- **Break-In Essentials** — professional resume rewrite + LinkedIn profile revamp; "fixes your story on paper the way hiring managers recognize it."
- **Full Career Package** — resume + LinkedIn + personalized strengths/gaps report + 90-day breakthrough plan + custom roadmap.

**Content pattern:** Blog + guides framed as insider documents ("How to Break Into Medical Device Sales in 2026"). Heavy on the idea that there are **multiple doors** — associate programs, distributor roles, clinical specialist positions — matched to your background. This "you don't need the obvious door" framing is pure category-education.

**Journey arc pattern:** Active rep → documents the system as a "blueprint" → productizes into tiered packages

**Why it matters to MedSkills:** Two steals. (1) The **"there are multiple doors" framing** is directly your category-education job — name the non-obvious entry routes into MedTech (RA associate, clinical coordination, market access, medical affairs) the way they name distributor/associate/clinical-specialist roles. (2) The **"treat your career switch like a territory you're going to win"** metaphor is gold for your ICP-2 (the MR already thinks in territory/target language — IN.5 in your swipe file proves it lands).

### Medical Sales Mentors (and its category cluster: MedSalesCoach, Medical Sales Business Coach)

**What it is:** A small "Medical Sales Mentors" company page exists, but the *model* it represents is a crowded US cluster of **community-as-product** mentorships. Because the specific brand is thin, I researched the cluster it sits in, since that's where the transferable pattern lives.
**The shared model across the cluster:**

- **Mentorship-until-hired:** MedSalesCoach runs "until you land a medical sales job — most clients finish in 60–90 days," claiming 95% placement and 30+ clients. Payment plans/financing offered.
- **High-touch cadence:** Medical Sales Business Coach sends a "Sales Tip" + "Motivational Text/Video" *every weekday* plus weekly 1-on-1 calls. The product is *frequency of contact*, not a video library.
- **Community as the asset:** "a community of hundreds we've helped break in." Belonging is the retention mechanic.

**Journey arc pattern:** Practitioner → 1:1 mentor → packages the mentorship + community → "we stay with you until you're hired"

**Why it matters to MedSkills:** This is the **community/accountability** lane. The lesson is that high-frequency, low-cost touch (a daily tip, a weekly check-in) builds more trust and retention than a one-time content drop. Maps directly onto the Batch-1 recommendation to run a "30-Day MedTech Knowledge Sprint." ⚠️ Note the "until you're hired / 95% placement" claims — exactly the outcome-guarantee language your compliance rules forbid until you have verified cohort data.

### Bada Business — Dr. Vivek Bindra

**Scale:** YouTube channel cited as the world's most-subscribed entrepreneurship/leadership channel at **22.5M+ subscribers**; Bada Business company LinkedIn ~63k followers; Vivek runs a large personal LinkedIn presence as the founder-face. 11 world records (9 Guinness) for largest webinars — scale itself is the marketing.
**Positioning:** Monk-turned-entrepreneur; "Business Coach to 100+ Top CEOs." The persona is the product.
**Content model:** **Hindi-first, problem-solving courses** for entrepreneurs/solopreneurs/students — finance, lead-gen, digital marketing, "how to start a startup." Courses framed as solving "burning problems," designed by people who built large organizations. The hook is accessibility (Hindi, relatable, mass-market), not exclusivity.

**Journey arc pattern:** Motivational speaker → YouTube mass-scale → founder-face brand → productized Hindi business courses

**Why it matters to MedSkills:** Bada Business defines **the ceiling and the risk of founder-face EdTech in India.** The upside: a single charismatic Hindi-speaking face (your Gagan) can carry enormous reach and trust. The cautionary half: Bindra's brand took serious reputational damage from controversy and over-hype/"get-rich" energy — the precise *opposite* of your "honesty without hype, no fake outcomes" mandate. **Model the Hinglish accessibility and founder-face leverage; reject the hype/guru energy.** Your brand brief already names this tension — Bada Business is the live case study of what happens when founder-face scale tips into hype.

### GrowthX

**What it is:** An **invite-only club** started in 2019 as a Sunday brainstorming group for founders/marketers/product people. Now a private community of **4,000+ members** who lead product/marketing/growth at Apple, CRED, Microsoft, Google, Netflix, Zerodha, etc. 95%+ are full-time professionals.
**Model:** Exclusivity *is* the marketing. Admission is gated (they screen for "empathy quotient"), which makes membership a status signal. Value delivered through: learning programs taught by operators who've actually scaled, a no-fluff private community, and in-person roundtables/mixers across 11 Indian cities. Recently pivoted messaging toward "stop talking, start implementing AI."

**Journey arc pattern:** Free Sunday group → invite-only club → status-gated premium membership → in-person + AI-implementation positioning

**Why it matters to MedSkills:** GrowthX is the **community-as-moat / scarcity** play. The transferable insight is *curation as a trust signal* — "we don't let everyone in" makes the people who are in feel chosen, and the alumni become your marketing. For MedSkills this is a longer-horizon move (you need a critical mass of credible members first), but the seed is usable now: position cohorts as *selective* ("we interview before we admit"), which both raises perceived value and protects outcomes (better-fit students place better). ⚠️ It also works *against* mass top-of-funnel reach — exclusivity and "teach everyone the category exists" pull in opposite directions. Use scarcity for the paid cohort, not for the awareness content.

## 2. NEW POST FORMATS (ready to model) — extends Batch 1's A–F

### Format G — The "Multiple Doors" Category Map (MedTech Blueprint model)

**Pattern:**
[Most people think there's one way in: the obvious role] →
[There are actually 3–5 doors most people never see] →
[Name each door + the background that fits it] →
[Which door fits you?]

**Why it works:** It does your top-of-funnel job (paths exist) *and* segments the reader by background in one post.
**MedSkills adaptation:** *"Everyone trying to leave pharma field sales applies for the same role. There are at least 4 doors into corporate MedTech most MRs never see: Regulatory Affairs associate, Clinical coordination, Market access, Medical affairs. Here's the background that fits each one."*

### Format H — The "Treat It Like a Territory" Reframe (MedTech Blueprint × ICP-2)

**Pattern:**
[Your career switch is failing because you're treating it like a lottery] →
[Treat it like a sales territory: research, target accounts, tailor the pitch, follow up] →
[The 6-phase plan] →
[Soft CTA]

**Why it works:** Speaks ICP-2's native language (territory, target, follow-up — see IN.5). Turns job-hunting anxiety into a familiar, winnable process.

### Format I — The "Advocate, Not Salesman" POV Post (Steve Adamson model)

**Pattern:**
[A small, real observation from inside the industry] →
[Encouragement to the person trying to break in] →
[No pitch, no link — just advocacy]
**Why it works:** It's the anti-funnel post. Builds the "this person isn't selling me" trust your cynical ICP-2 requires. This is a *cadence* play — sprinkle these between your structured posts so the feed doesn't feel like a campaign.

### Format J — The Selective-Cohort Signal (GrowthX model)

**Pattern:**
[We interview before we admit] →
[Here's who we say no to and why] →
[What that protects for the people who get in]
**Why it works:** Scarcity + standards = perceived value + a trust signal that you're not just taking anyone's ₹20k. ⚠️ Use only for paid-cohort posts, never awareness posts.

## 4. UPDATED KEY STRATEGIC INSIGHT

Batch 1's headline was: *make your placement data public (Kraftshala).* This batch sharpens it into a sequencing rule, because two of these profiles sit at opposite ends of a real tension:

- **GrowthX** says: be exclusive, curated, scarce.
- **Bada Business** says: be mass, accessible, everywhere.

You can't be both in the same channel. The resolution that fits your model: **be Bada Business (accessible, Hinglish, founder-face, mass reach) in your awareness content, and GrowthX (selective, "we interview before we admit") in your paid-cohort positioning.** Steve Adamson's advocate tone is the connective tissue that keeps the mass-reach content from feeling like a funnel.

The one move you can make *this week* with zero compliance risk: adopt **Steve Adamson's "advocate, not salesman" POV posts** as a recurring format. It builds the exact trust your ICP-2 is cynical about, requires no cohort data, and costs nothing but Gagan's authentic voice.
