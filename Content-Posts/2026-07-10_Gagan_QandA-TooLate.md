## POST

In the MedTech 101 Q&A, one question came up more than any other.

"Sir, is it too late for me?"

It came from MRs seven years in, from people who'd done an M.Pharm and then felt stuck, from a 36-year-old who assumed the window had shut at 30.

Here's the honest answer I gave live. No, it isn't too late, but "too late" is the wrong thing to be worried about.

I've hired into device companies for 25 years, and I've never once passed on someone for being 34 instead of 24. What gets people passed over is showing up with pharma experience written in pharma language, hoping the hiring manager will translate it for them.

Your age isn't the problem. The way the experience is framed is, and that part you can fix.

A 32-year-old MR who can explain how their territory and clinical relationships map onto a device role will beat a 24-year-old with a tidy CV and nothing behind it. Years in the field count for a lot once you know how to present them.

So the question isn't whether you're too late. It's whether you've learned to position what you already have.

For the experienced folks here: what's the fear actually sitting behind "is it too late"? Say it plainly below and I'll answer honestly. 👇
