Part 1: The Algorithm

The Front Window

  • Initial Testing Phase: When you post something, LinkedIn first shows it to only a small handful of people.
  • The Evaluation Loop: Then it watches what those people do.
  • Positive Signal: If they stop and actually read it, LinkedIn thinks this is good and shows it to many more people.
  • Negative Signal: If they scroll past quickly, LinkedIn quietly buries it, and almost no one sees it.
    • The Core Objective

    "So the whole game is:
    Make people stop and read."

    Part 1: The Algorithm

    What LinkedIn Quietly Hides

    The algorithm is designed to penalize content that lowers user retention or feels unnatural.

    🔗

    Posts with a link inside them

    LinkedIn wants to keep users on their platform. Outbound links in the post body result in heavily restricted organic distribution.

    #️⃣

    Posts with too many hashtags

    Excessive hashtag clouds look spammy. They dilute relevance, triggering automated reach suppression filters.

    🤖

    Posts that feel copy-pasted or AI-written

    Standard AI-generated structures, overly structured bullet lists, and corporate phrasing are heavily deprioritized in the feed.

    👥

    The same post coming from two accounts

    Duplicated text is treated as spam. Direct reposting or copying verbatim splits the algorithm's focus and lowers overall impact.

    Maintaining Content Integrity

    "If Shilpi ma'am’s post could easily become Gagan sir’s post, we change it."

    This rule guarantees that we do not write generic content that could belong to anyone. It forces specificity, which drives reading time and authentic engagement.

    The Rationale

    When everyone has a separate, distinct voice, we cover three different perspectives of the exact same journey.

    Instead of boring, repetitive copy, our audience sees a multi-dimensional startup being built in real time.

    Part 4: Rules

    Rules of Engagement (1 – 4)

    01

    Write like a human talking to a friend

    Avoid corporate templates, marketing slogans, or dry press-release formats. Write as if talking directly to a peer.

    02

    No links inside the post

    Never include links in the body. If someone wants details or registration info, direct them to send a DM or reply in comments.

    03

    Maximum 5 hashtags

    Less hashtags equal better reach in 2026. Keep tags minimal and highly targeted to protect page authority.

    04

    Reply to every comment in the first hour

    Always reply to early comments as a real human. Early comment-reply chains signals the algorithm to distribute widely.

    Part 4: Rules

    Rules of Engagement (5 – 7)

    05

    Post steadily, not randomly

    Consistency builds algorithmic favor. Four highly polished posts spread evenly across the week beats random, sporadic bursts of content.

    06

    Never post a result we cannot prove

    Honesty is our brand's greatest edge. If we publish a stat or success metric, it must be 100% auditable. We do not compromise on this.

    07

    No two people post the same thing

    Keep our narrative fresh. When we share a theme, each account must tackle it from their own perspective. Never double-post text.
