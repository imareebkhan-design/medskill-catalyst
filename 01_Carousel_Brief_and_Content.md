### Slides 2–8 — ONE ROLE EACH

**ROLE 01 — Medical Device Sales / Territory Manager**  *(slide 2 — box explains the role + day-to-day, not "why pharma counts")*
- What it is: The company's face in hospitals — selling medical devices to surgeons, doctors and procurement teams across your assigned territory.
- What the day-to-day looks like: Visiting hospitals and operating theatres, demoing devices and supporting live cases, building doctor and KOL relationships, negotiating with procurement, and tracking your territory's pipeline against targets. Relationship-driven, field-heavy work.
- Difficulty: **Low (2/5)** · Engineering: **No** · *The obvious door — which is why it's competitive.*

**ROLE 02 — Clinical Research Associate / Trials Coordinator**  ⭐ *RIBBON: UNDERRATED — one of the 2 easiest*
- What it is: Running clinical evaluations and device trials end to end.
- Why your pharma background counts: Protocols, clinical data, GCP — this is the language your pharma background already gave you. Field experience or fresh degree, you start ahead.
- Difficulty: **Very low (1/5)** · Engineering: **No**

**ROLE 03 — Regulatory Affairs Associate**  ⭐ *RIBBON: UNDERRATED — one of the 2 easiest*
- What it is: Getting devices approved and compliant — CDSCO, MDR, technical files.
- Why your pharma background counts: Approvals, labels, compliance — pharma logic maps straight onto devices. Years in the field or straight from your studies, you're not starting from zero.
- Difficulty: **Low (2/5)** · Engineering: **No**

**ROLE 04 — Clinical Application Specialist**
- What it is: Training doctors and staff to use the device in the clinical environment.
- Why your pharma background counts: You understand the clinical environment and how to explain a product to doctors — half this role. The gap is device-technical depth, learnable from a field or academic start.
- Difficulty: **Moderate (3/5)** · Engineering: **Not mandatory, but technical/clinical familiarity preferred**

**ROLE 05 — Market Access / Health Economics (HEOR)**
- What it is: Pricing, reimbursement and the economic case for a device.
- Why your pharma background counts: You already grasp how price, value and access decide whether a product wins. Pharma exposure — field or academic — gives the instinct; you add the data skills.
- Difficulty: **High (4/5)** · Engineering: **No**

**ROLE 06 — Quality / Design Assurance Engineer**
- What it is: Owning product quality systems and design controls (ISO 13485, CAPA).
- Why your pharma background counts: Your pharma and quality knowledge gives a head start — but this track usually expects an engineering or technical degree. Be realistic before you aim here.
- Difficulty: **High (4/5)** · Engineering: **Usually required**

**ROLE 07 — R&D / Product Development Engineer**
- What it is: Designing and building the device itself.
- Why your pharma background counts: The hardest door for any pharma background — field or fresh. An engineering degree is the real prerequisite, so go in clear-eyed.
- Difficulty: **Very high (5/5)** · Engineering: **Required**

## 4. What changed after verification (so you can defend it)
- **"2 easiest, non-obvious" = Clinical Research + Regulatory** (was wrongly Clinical Application Specialist). Both explicitly list pharma/life-science as core eligibility.
- **Clinical Application Specialist downgraded to Moderate** — it often prefers biomedical-engineering/clinical backgrounds. Framed honestly so a knowledgeable viewer can't poke a hole in it.
- **"2 need engineering" = Quality/Design Assurance + R&D.** The other 5 don't *mandate* an engineering degree (caption claim holds).
- **No salary figures.** Difficulty is editorial assessment, labelled as such on every slide.
