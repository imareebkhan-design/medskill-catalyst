# MedSkills Catalyst Brochure — Design & Copy Critique + Revision Prompt

## Part 1 — The critique

### A. The naming problem (your instinct is right, and it's worse than one word)

The document uses **four different words for the same relationship**:

| Page | Word used |
|---|---|
| Cover / p5 | "Career **Advisory**" |
| p2 | "A **mentor** who stays past the first offer" |
| p5, p6 | "your **advisor**" |
| p6 | "Career **Coaching**" (inside the Career Accelerator tier) |

Two of these actively contradict each other. Page 5 asks *"Is this coaching or advisory?"* and answers *"Advisory."* — then page 6 sells "Career Coaching" as a line item. A careful reader catches that in ten seconds.

**Why "advisor" specifically fails here.** In Indian MedTech, "advisor" reads as a board advisor or a part-time consultant — someone attached to a company, not someone accountable for your outcome. Worse, *"your advisor"* used generically implies there is a **bench of advisors**. There isn't. There's Gagan. When the buyer works that out, the brochure looks like it was inflating.

**Recommended fix — two moves:**

1. **Title:** *Gagan Victor — Founder & MedTech Career Strategist.* "Strategist" describes the function (he builds strategy) without claiming a credential, is standard usage across career services globally, and matches what the programme actually delivers: a written strategy, not motivation. It also sits naturally next to "advisory" as the engagement type.

   Alternatives if you want a different register:
   - *Career Strategist, MedTech* — cleanest, most neutral
   - *Practice Lead, Career Advisory* — more institutional, slightly cold
   - Avoid: Expert, Guru, Career Guide, Career Architect (all overclaim or read soft)

2. **In running copy, use his name — not a role noun.** Replace *"sessions with your advisor"* with *"sessions with Gagan"*. This turns the solo-practice weakness into the actual product: you get the founder in every session, not a junior on rotation. That is a genuinely premium proposition and right now the brochure hides it.

3. Then **standardise the vocabulary**: the engagement is **advisory**, the person is a **strategist**, and **mentorship** is reserved only for stage 5 and the quarterly add-on, where it accurately means the ongoing post-programme relationship. Delete "coaching" entirely — rename the Career Accelerator line item to *"Career Strategy Sessions"*.

### B. Ship-blockers (fix before this goes anywhere)

1. **Page 5 contains a visible internal note:** *"To add before print: years in MedTech, companies worked at, and one line on the sectors covered."* This is printed in the PDF, styled as a callout. If this reached a prospect, it says the brochure was shipped unfinished.

2. **The Gagan bio is empty of substance.** *"Advises MedTech professionals across India on career strategy, positioning and progression."* — that's a restatement of the programme, not a reason to trust him. Needs: years in MedTech, companies, functions covered, and roughly how many professionals he's worked with. This is the single highest-value copy edit in the document. The entire proposition rests on one person's judgment and there is currently no evidence offered for it.

3. **No photograph of Gagan, and no human presence anywhere in seven pages.** A brochure selling one-to-one personal advisory with zero human imagery is a trust gap, not a minimalism choice.

4. **Zero social proof.** No testimonial, no outcome, no company logos, no number. Seven pages of claims with nothing behind them.

5. **Seven pages won't print as a booklet.** Saddle-stitched work must be a multiple of four. This needs to be 8 pages — which is convenient, because the fixes below need the room.

---

### C. Layout — the dominant design failure

**Content pools at the top of every page and leaves a dead zone at the bottom.** Measured roughly:

| Page | Dead vertical space |
|---|---|
| 1 (cover) | ~45% — a void between the title block and the hero line |
| 2 | ~30% — a gap between "What Changes" and "Who It's For" |
| 3 | ~25% below stage 5 |
| 4 | gap between the card grid and "The Transformation" |
| 5 | ~15% |
| 7 | ~30% — a void between the add-on list and the CTA |

This is not premium white space. Premium white space is **distributed** — around and between elements, in consistent proportion. Pooled white space at the bottom of a page reads as *unfinished layout*, because that is what it usually is. Right now every page looks like the content ran out.

**Fix:** set a baseline grid and either (a) let content occupy the full type area with balanced leading, or (b) deliberately compose the empty space — anchor it with a rule, a pull-quote, or a colour block so it reads as intentional.

### D. Other design gaps

6. **Every page opens identically.** Eyebrow rule → eyebrow caps → large headline → grey sub-line → content. Seven times. There is no rhythm, no change of pace, no moment where the eye is surprised. A premium brochure varies pacing — at minimum one full-bleed dark spread and one large pull-quote to break the pattern.

7. **The cover is under-designed.** Beautiful gradient, but the middle 500px is empty and the hero line is orphaned at the bottom with no relationship to the title block. Either move the hero line up under the sub-headline, or place a visual anchor in the middle third.

8. **The icon set on page 2 is semantically arbitrary.** A sun for "Engineers & pharma professionals," a bar chart for "Sales & Product." The icons carry no meaning and vary in optical weight and stroke. Either commission a coherent five-icon set on a shared grid, or drop icons entirely and use numbered labels — cleaner and more honest.

9. **Pricing cards on page 6 are structurally broken.** The "Most Popular" badge sits *inside* the card and pushes the Career Accelerator content down, so the four cards no longer align horizontally. Prices wrap to two lines ("₹4,999 –" / "₹7,999"), which looks like a rendering error. Card heights are content-driven rather than normalised. Fix: equal-height cards, badge tabbed above the card edge, prices set to fit one line.

10. **The add-on list on page 7 is unbalanced** — 4 items left, 3 right, leaving a floating rule under the right column with nothing beneath it. And "₹12,000 / quarter" is longer than every other price, breaking the right alignment. Rebalance to 4/3 with a deliberate terminator, or 5/2 with a note.

11. **The Before/After block doesn't do its job.** The brief asked for instant contrast; the grey and navy are too close in value to read as opposition at a glance. Push the contrast — muted grey at 40% opacity vs full navy, or invert the "After" panel entirely.

12. **One accent colour is doing all the work.** No secondary tone means no mid-level hierarchy — everything is either navy, blue, or grey. Add one tint level to separate structural elements from emphasis.

13. **Inconsistent measure between adjacent columns on page 2** — the left column runs ~48 characters, the right ~62. Set both to the same measure.

14. **The footer logo roundel is too small to survive print.** A circular mark with text set inside it needs ~15mm minimum diameter. At the current footer size the wordmark inside will fill in.

---

### E. Copy and tone gaps

15. **"Premium Deliverables" / "Premium Add-Ons."** Premium brands do not describe themselves as premium. It reads as reassurance-seeking. Use "What You Leave With" and "Add-Ons" — the price and the design should carry the premium signal, not an adjective.

16. **"Investment" instead of "Price" or "Fee."** This is a coaching-industry euphemism and Indian buyers read it as evasive. "Fee" is more direct and, counterintuitively, more premium — confident sellers name a price.

17. **Price ranges signal that the price is negotiable.** "₹4,999 – ₹7,999" invites a haggle and tells the reader you haven't decided what this is worth. Quote a single number per track; handle variation in the discovery call.

18. **Spelling system is inconsistent.** The document uses British forms — *Personalised, Optimisation, Instalment* — but page 1 says "Program" while page 7 says "programme." Pick one. Simplest fix: keep "Program" everywhere (it's part of the product name) and change page 7's "programme" to "program."

19. **"Advanced Module" means nothing to a buyer.** Advanced relative to what? If there's no publicly known foundation module, this label creates a question instead of answering one. Either explain it in four words on the cover, or drop it.

20. **Page 2's headline leads with the reader's failure.** *"Many MedTech professionals feel stuck despite strong technical capability."* True, but it's the first content page and it opens by telling the buyer what's wrong with them. Also "feel stuck" is soft. Consider leading with the mechanism or the outcome and demoting the pain to the sub-line.

21. **The tagline "Upskill to Upscale" is grammatically wrong.** "Upscale" is an adjective meaning high-end; it isn't a verb meaning to grow. The intended word is "scale." This lives in the logo so it's a separate decision, but a native-English reader will notice it, and MedTech has a lot of them.

22. **Check apostrophes and quote marks are typographic (' ') not straight (' ").** "Don't," "you're," "What's," "isn't," "It's" all appear to be using straight apostrophes. At this level of design polish, that's a visible flaw.

23. **What genuinely works — keep it:** the "Where it stalls / What changes" structure on page 2 is sharp, specific writing. "Five stages, in order" is confident. And *"We do not place candidates and we do not guarantee a job offer. No program can."* is the strongest copy in the document — it buys more trust than any claim on any other page. Consider giving it more prominence rather than parking it at the bottom of page 5.

### 1. Terminology — apply globally, this is the priority change

The document currently uses four different words for the same relationship. Standardise:

- **The engagement** is *advisory*. Keep "Personalised Career Advisory."
- **The person** is a *strategist*. Gagan Victor's title everywhere is **"Founder & MedTech Career Strategist."**
- **In running copy, use his name, never a generic role noun.** Replace every instance of *"your advisor"* with *"Gagan"* or *"Gagan Victor."* Specifically:
  - Page 5: "You work directly with your advisor through all five stages" → "You work directly with Gagan through all five stages"
  - Page 6, All Tracks Include: "One-to-one sessions with your advisor" → "One-to-one sessions with Gagan Victor"
- **Delete the word "coaching" entirely.** On page 6, rename the Career Accelerator inclusion from "Career Coaching" to **"Career Strategy Sessions."** This currently contradicts the page 5 FAQ.
- **Reserve "mentor" / "mentorship"** for stage 5 (Career Partnership) and the quarterly add-on only — where it accurately means the ongoing post-programme relationship. Remove it from page 2; change *"A mentor who stays past the first offer"* to **"Someone who stays past the first offer."**

### 2. Remove the internal note

Page 5 currently prints a visible production note: *"To add before print: years in MedTech, companies worked at, and one line on the sectors covered."* Delete it. Replace the Gagan bio with a substantive three-line credential block covering years in MedTech, companies, functions and sectors covered, and approximate number of professionals advised. Leave clearly marked placeholder fields ONLY if the copy is not yet supplied — and style them as obvious editable placeholders, never as finished body copy.

Add space in this block for a **professional photograph of Gagan**, treated in the brand style — square or circular crop, brand-colour duotone or a clean neutral background. This is a personal advisory service; the brochure needs a human face.

### 3. Fix the vertical rhythm — the most important layout change

Every page currently loads content into the top 60–70% and leaves dead space at the bottom. Pooled white space at the bottom of a page reads as unfinished, not premium.

- Establish a consistent baseline grid across all pages.
- Distribute white space between elements rather than letting it collect at the foot of the page.
- Every page's content should either fill the type area with balanced leading, or the empty space must be deliberately composed — anchored by a rule, a pull-quote, or a colour block.
- Page 1 (cover): the hero line *"Don't just change jobs. Build the right MedTech career."* is orphaned at the bottom, ~500px below the title block. Either raise it directly beneath the sub-headline, or introduce a visual anchor in the middle third of the page.
- Page 7: close the void between the add-on list and the CTA band. Make it one continuous composition.

### 4. Break the visual monotony

All seven pages currently open with the identical module: eyebrow rule → eyebrow caps → headline → grey sub-line → content. Introduce pacing:

- Make **one page a full-bleed dark spread** in the cover's navy — the strongest candidate is the honesty statement. Give *"We do not place candidates and we do not guarantee a job offer. No program can. What we build is your roadmap, your positioning and your readiness — the offer comes from your own market."* a full dark page as a large pull-quote. It is the most trust-building copy in the document and it is currently buried at the foot of page 5.
- Use the extra eighth page for this, or for a testimonial page.

### 5. Component-level fixes

**Page 2**
- Set the left and right columns to the same measure (currently ~48 vs ~62 characters).
- The five audience icons are semantically arbitrary and inconsistent in stroke weight. Either redraw them as a coherent set on a shared grid with matched optical weight, or remove icons entirely and use a clean numbered or ruled list.

**Page 4**
- Retitle "Premium Deliverables" → **"What You Leave With."**
- Increase the Before/After contrast so it reads at a glance: "Before" in grey at ~40% opacity, "After" in full navy — or invert the After panel to a filled brand-colour block with reversed white type.

**Page 6**
- Normalise all four pricing cards to **equal height** with matched internal padding.
- Move the "Most Popular" badge so it tabs **above** the card's top edge rather than sitting inside and pushing that card's content down. All four card headings must align on the same horizontal.
- Prices must fit on **one line** — reduce the type size or widen the cards so "₹4,999 – ₹7,999" does not wrap.
- Change the label "INVESTMENT" to **"FEE."**

**Page 7**
- Retitle "Premium Add-Ons" → **"Add-Ons."**
- Rebalance the two-column price list. There is currently a floating rule under the right column with no row beneath it. Set both columns to the same number of rows, or add a deliberate terminating rule.
- Right-align all prices to a common axis and handle "₹12,000 / quarter" so it does not break that alignment — set "/ quarter" smaller and beneath the figure if needed.

**All pages**
- Increase the footer logo roundel to a minimum reproducible size (~15mm diameter) or replace it with a simplified wordmark. At the current size the type inside the circle will fill in when printed.
- Introduce one mid-tone tint between the navy and the light grey, so structural elements and emphasis are not competing at the same value.

### 6. Copy edits

- Page 7: change "programme" to "program" for consistency with the rest of the document.
- Verify all apostrophes and quotation marks are typographic (' ') and not straight (' "). Check "Don't," "you're," "What's," "isn't," "It's."
- Cover: either add four words clarifying what "Advanced Module" means, or remove the label.

### 7. Insert social proof

The document currently contains no testimonial, outcome, statistic, or client logo. Add:

- **One short testimonial** (name, role, company) placed on page 2 or 4, styled as a quiet pull-quote — not a boxed "review" widget.
- **A trust strip** of company logos where past clients now work, if available, along the foot of page 2.
- Mark both as clearly-styled placeholders if the content is not yet supplied.

### 8. Output

Export as a print-ready 8-page PDF, CMYK-safe, with selectable text and live links, plus a screen-optimised version under 5MB for WhatsApp and email.
