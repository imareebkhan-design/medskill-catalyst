# PART 1 — SET UP (do this once, 10 minutes)

You only set up once. All three videos use the same setup. Nothing changes between them except the words.

### Your 10-point checklist

| # | What | Details |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Phone on a tripod** | Rear camera (the better one, on the back). Never hold it in your hand. If you don't have a tripod, stack books and lean the phone against a wall. |
| 2 | **Turn the phone vertical** | Portrait, not landscape. Very important — we'll crop it later, but we can't un-crop it. |
| 3 | **Collar mic** | Please don't skip this. A ₹500 clip-on mic makes more difference than a ₹50,000 camera. Clip it just below your collarbone. |
| 4 | **Quiet room** | Fan off. AC off. Windows closed. A room with a sofa, curtains or carpet sounds much better than an empty room. |
| 5 | **Face a window** | Daylight on your face is the best light there is. Don't stand *with* the window behind you — you'll come out as a black shape. No direct sunlight either. |
| 6 | **Frame yourself waist-up** | Not just your face. Waist-up, so your hands come into the picture when you talk. |
| 7 | **Eyes in the top third** | Leave empty space above your head. We're going to put a headline banner there. |
| 8 | **Plain background** | A blank wall is perfect. Avoid: bed, kitchen, cluttered bookshelf, any visible brand names or posters. |
| 9 | **Shirt: solid navy blue or plain white** | **No stripes, no checks, no small patterns** — they flicker badly on a phone camera. Collar shirt, no tie. |
| 10 | **Look at the lens, not the screen** | This is the one everyone gets wrong. Talk to the little glass circle. If it helps, stick a small arrow or a sticky note next to it. |

### Two things that matter more than everything above

**Do not say hello.** No "Hi everyone", no "Namaste", no "I'm Gagan, founder of MedSkills Catalyst". People scroll past introductions. Your very first word is the first word of the script. You can introduce yourself later, or not at all.

**Record each script three times.** First take you're stiff. Second take you're thinking about the words. Third take you sound like yourself. **We will almost always use take three.** So please don't stop after one good-sounding attempt.

# PART 2 — THE THREE SCRIPTS

> **A note on who you're talking to.**
> Picture one person: a final-year B.Pharm student, or someone who graduated eight months ago and is still job-hunting. They've been told there are two options — become a medical representative, or study further. They have never heard the word "MedTech." They are not stupid; they're just uninformed, and slightly worried.
>
> You are not selling to them. You are telling them something useful that nobody told them. That's the tone for all three videos.

## SCRIPT 1 — "The Third Option"

> **Total time: about 55 seconds. This is our main video — record this one first, while you're freshest.**

> **DELIVERY — first 5 seconds. Say this fast and flat, like you're stating a fact. No smiling into it, no warm-up.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"If you're in your final year of B.Pharm or M.Pharm, you've been told there are two options.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Become a medical representative. Or study further.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**There's a third one, and almost nobody mentions it."**

> **PAUSE HERE. A full one-second stop. This is where we'll make a cut in the edit — we need the gap.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"It's called MedTech. Medical devices.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Implants, surgical instruments, diagnostic machines, patient monitoring systems. It's a completely separate industry from pharma, and it's growing in India."**

> **DELIVERY — slow down from here. This next part is the important bit. This is you teaching, not announcing. Lean in slightly.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"And here's the part that matters for you.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**These companies are not looking for engineers. They're looking for people who already understand clinical language — anatomy, physiology, terminology, how a hospital actually works.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**That is exactly what your degree gave you.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**You are already qualified. You just don't have the map."**

> **PAUSE. One second.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"That's what our Foundation Module is.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**The full device landscape — diagnostic, surgical, implantable, monitoring. How Class A to Class D risk classification works. Who manufactures what in India. And where the high-value roles actually sit — because it is not only sales."**

> **DELIVERY — CTA. Calm and straightforward. Don't get excited here. You're offering, not pushing.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"I run a free masterclass where I walk through all of this live.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**No MedTech experience needed. That's the whole point.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Link is below. Come and see if this is for you."**

> **END. Hold still and quiet for two seconds before you reach for the phone — the editor needs clean tail footage.**

## SCRIPT 2 — "Nine Doors"

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"Your college prepared you for one job.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**The medical device industry has nine."**

> **PAUSE — one full second.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"Medical device sales.**
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Clinical roles.**
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Product and application specialist.**
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Marketing.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Market access.**

## SCRIPT 3 — "Nobody Told Them"

> **Total time: about 55 seconds. This one is you speaking personally. Slowest and warmest of the three.**

> **DELIVERY — this is the one where you sound like yourself, not like an ad. Almost like you're telling a colleague something that bothers you.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"I've spent years in the medical device industry.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**And the thing that still gets to me is this — I keep meeting pharmacy graduates who would have been excellent at this work. Who never applied.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Not because they weren't good enough. Because nobody ever told them the industry existed."**

> **PAUSE — one full second. Let that land.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"Think about what you were actually taught.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Nobody explained what happens inside a cath lab. Or what CSSD stands for. Or how many people sign off before a hospital buys a single implant.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**People learn all of that after they get the job. Or they get rejected before they ever get the chance to learn it."**

> **DELIVERY — bridge. Straighten up a little here, the tone shifts from concerned to practical.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"That's the exact gap the Foundation Module was built for.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"This is not a job guarantee. I'm never going to tell you that.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**It's preparation. So that when the opportunity comes, you're not learning on the spot."**

> **DELIVERY — CTA. Quiet, unhurried.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"There's a free masterclass where I walk through the whole module.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Give it one hour. At the very least you'll walk away knowing what's out there.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Link is below."**

> This section is not for the recording. It's for whoever cuts the video.

### Brand rules that must not be broken

Taken from `BRAND.md`. These are contrast and identity rules, not preferences.

- Cyan `#4AD0FF` is an **accent only** — a thin underline or a small shape. **Never cyan text on a light background. Never white text on cyan.** Both fail accessibility contrast.
- **One blue CTA per frame.** Not two.
- **Light-dominant.** Roughly 60% of the frame is canvas or white. Never a wall of blue.
- Logo sits on a quiet background only — white, pale blue `#E8F2FB`, or navy. **Never over the video.**
- No black drop shadows, no glows, no gradients.
- Fonts: **Fraunces** for headlines only. **Plus Jakarta Sans** for everything else. Never swap them.

### Edit checklist

1. **Cut the first breath.** Trim to the first consonant of the first word. Zero dead air at the start.
2. **Burned-in captions on every video.** Most of this audience watches on mute. Plus Jakarta Sans 600, white, subtle dark scrim behind, 2–4 words per card, tightly synced. This is the single highest-impact thing in the edit.
3. **One punch-in.** A 10% scale-up at the "teaching" moment (Script 1: "here's the part that matters" / Script 2: the "not your fault" line / Script 3: "this is not a job guarantee"). One only — more than one feels frantic.
4. **Script 2 needs on-screen text for the nine roles** — one word appearing as he says each. This is what makes Script 2 work.
5. **Music:** none, or ambient at about 10%. The competitor ads that are working have effectively no music. The voice carries it.
6. **End card:** 2 seconds. Logo on pale blue `#E8F2FB`, one line — "Free Masterclass · Register" — one blue button. Nothing else on it.
7. **Export three sizes from the same master:** 9:16 (Reels and Stories), 4:5 (main feed — this is the volume driver), 1:1 (backup). Keep face and captions inside the safe area on all three.

# PART 7 — HOW WE'LL KNOW WHAT WORKED

Run all three at equal budget for five days.

Look at **3-second view rate first**, not clicks. That number tells you which *opening line* is working, which is the only thing you can act on. Then look at cost per registration.

Best guess: **Script 1** will get the most volume because it speaks to the widest anxiety. **Script 2** will probably have the lowest cost per registration — a list creates a real curiosity gap, and people who don't recognise the roles are exactly the people who need the module. **Script 3** is the one to show people who've already seen the others; it's the most credible and least pushy, which is what works on a second exposure.

Whichever wins, keep the frame and change only the headline next round. That's the pattern behind every competitor ad set we looked at — a small number of strong ideas, refreshed constantly, never rebuilt from scratch.
