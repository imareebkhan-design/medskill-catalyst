### Post 1.12 — Jacob McLaughlin · *10 mistakes when breaking in* ⭐ (high-value educational)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7470444664555458560/

```
👉🏽10 Mistakes To Avoid When Breaking Into Medical Device Sales

1. Don't Say "I Want to Pick Your Brain": This industry is all about professionalism. Saying "I want to pick your brain" in your messages? It doesn't scream pro. Trust me, I've been there, and those messages got crickets. Keep it professional and stand out for the right reasons.

2. Watch Your Names in Messages: It's easy to mess this up when you're copying and pasting on LinkedIn. Once, I sent a message with the wrong name—talk about embarrassing! Always double-check who you're talking to and what day it is. No more "Happy Tuesday" on a Wednesday!

3. Don't Come Unprepared: I used to jump on calls without really knowing what medical device sales involved. Big mistake. Do your homework, understand who you're talking to, and what their role involves. It makes a difference.

4. Don't Put All Your Eggs in One Basket: Focusing on just one company can backfire big time. They might hit a hiring freeze, and where does that leave you? Diversify your options to keep your options open.

5. Know Who's Who: Understanding the difference between a territory manager and a regional sales manager is crucial. You don't want to mix them up during your interactions because who you're talking to might just be deciding your future with the company.

6. Don't Wing Conversations: Preparation is key. You can't just rely on being a smooth talker. Practicing your pitches and understanding your product inside out can set you apart in this competitive field.

7. Ignore Bad Advice: Be wary of where you're getting your advice from. Not everyone giving advice is performing at the top of their game. Stick to advice from those who are truly successful and are where you want to be.

8. Follow Up Wisely: Don't harass people with endless follow-ups. If someone isn't responding after a couple of attempts, they're probably not going to. Focus your energy on those who are engaging with you.

9. Don't Overshare: Keep your conversations concise and to the point. You're here to make a professional impression, not to make a new best friend. Save the small talk for after you've nailed the job.

10. Don't Rely Solely on Drive: While being driven is important, it's not enough to just show enthusiasm. You need to demonstrate skills, knowledge, and the ability to meet the company's needs.

Final Thoughts: Breaking into medical device sales isn't just about who you know or making a good impression. 

It's about being prepared, staying professional, and learning from every interaction. If you're feeling stuck or unsure about your progress, remember that every misstep is a learning opportunity. Keep pushing, keep learning, and keep reaching out.....you'll find your breakthrough
```

## Pattern notes — Batch 2 (what to actually steal)

- **The "experience is your advantage, not your liability" reframe is the single most transferable hook for your ICP-2.** Jacob's "No Sales Experience No Problem" and Bagga's "luck vs strategy" both convert a feeling of *disqualification* into a *playable game*. For MedSkills: "Years as a Pharma MR isn't wasted experience — it's the exact head start MedTech device companies are hiring for. Most MRs just don't know how to position it."
- **List-format teardown posts travel** (Jacob's 10 Mistakes, Bagga's 4-point game-plan). High save/share rate, easy to localize: "7 reasons your pharma resume gets auto-rejected for corporate MedTech roles."
- **Masai's "Rahul — A FIGHTER" is your transformation-script template.** Structure: ordinary protagonist → systemic failure (cleared 11 written rounds, no job, never touched a computer in 4 yrs of CSE) → intervention → concrete outcome (3X package, broke college record) → "this is repeatable." Swap the engineering details for a B.Pharm/MR arc and you have a documentary script skeleton. Note the compliance discipline you'll need: Masai names a real student and real numbers — only do that with verified consent + data.
- **Founder build-in-public (Prateek's anniversary, Anshuman's "skill trumps everything") is your Bucket-2 lane that brand pages can't copy.** Beliefs stated plainly + named team + a contrarian truth. This is exactly the founder-honesty positioning in your brand brief.
- **Avoid the trap visible in Bagga's promo posts** (book/course CTA dumps). They get reach off his existing audience but read as pitches — the opposite of your "earn trust before selling" mandate.
```
