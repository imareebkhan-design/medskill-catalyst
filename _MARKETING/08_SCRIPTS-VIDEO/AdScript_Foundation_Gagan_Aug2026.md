## PART 1 — What is actually working in Anil Lamba's ads

### 1.1 The full picture

13 active ads. Only **3 distinct copy angles** and **2 creative formats**. That is the whole lesson: he is not making 13 ads, he is making 3 ideas and running them 4 ways each, refreshing every few days.

| Angle | Ads running | First line | Offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Fear statistic** | 5 | "8 out of 10 businesses fail due to poor financial decisions." | Think Like a CFO — 90 min |
| **B. Curriculum list** | 5 | "In this masterclass Dr Lamba will 1. … 2. … 3. … 4. …" | Think Like a CFO — 90 min |
| **C. Hidden leak** | 3 | "Revenue is one thing, but real freedom comes when you command the flow of funds." | Funds Flow — 3 hr, first 50 only |

Every single ad ends on scarcity ("Seats filling fast", "Only first 50", "Limited seats") and every single CTA is **Book now / Enrol Now**. Not "Learn more". Not "Sign up". A booking verb.

### 1.2 The creative formats

**Format 1 — Bare talking head (majority of ads).**
Dr. Lamba seated, mid-shot, plain office or study behind him, no b-roll, no graphics, no music bed you'd notice. Vertical 4:5 or 1:1. It looks like a man who knows something talking to you. That's the entire production value.

**Format 2 — Framed talking head (the higher-effort ones).** This is the one to copy. A static branded card wraps the video:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  Working 12-Hour Days But       │  ← headline, black + red keyword
│  Profits Standing Still?        │
│  ┌───────────────────────────┐  │
│  │                           │  │
│  │   TALKING HEAD VIDEO      │  │  ← the actual footage, centre band
│  │                           │  │
│  ├───────────────────────────┤  │
│  │ ₹1999 Workshop – Limited  │  │  ← offer strip, yellow
│  └───────────────────────────┘  │
│  Fix Your Cash Flow             │  ← outcome line
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

Second variant of the same frame: "Is Your Business Secretly **Bleeding Profits** Every Month?" / "Register Now & Find The Leaks".

**Why this frame wins:** the ad sells even on mute, even before the video loads, even in the 0.4 seconds before a thumb decides. The headline does the hooking; the video does the trusting. Most people never hear a word of it.

**Format 3 — Testimonial (only 1 ad).** A regular-looking man, striped shirt, plain bedroom wall, phone camera. Deliberately unpolished. It runs alongside the polished ones, not instead of them.

### 1.3 The four mechanics worth stealing

1. **A number that indicts, not impresses.** "8 out of 10 businesses fail" points at the viewer. Nobody advertises "we have 6 modules" — that's about the seller.
2. **Name the enemy as invisible.** "Bleeding profits", "cash leaks", "secretly". The viewer's problem is reframed as something happening *right now* that they cannot see. That is what buys the click — not the promise.
3. **Time-box the fix.** "90 minutes." "3 hours." "Half day." The commitment is stated before the value is. Removing the time objection early is doing more work here than any benefit claim.
4. **Kill the disqualifier out loud.** "Even if you're new to finance." His audience's private fear is *I won't understand it*. He answers it in the ad, not on the landing page.

### 1.4 What he does that we must NOT copy

His ads lean on hard scarcity and a fear statistic with no source. Our `BRAND.md` is explicit: no fabricated proof, no guarantees, Sage voice — "persuade by being clear and right, not loud." So we take his **structure** and reject his **temperature**. Concretely:

- ❌ "8 out of 10 fail" with no citation → ✅ a verifiable, sourced or self-evident observation
- ❌ "Only first 50!" → ✅ a real, true cohort cap
- ❌ guarantee language → ✅ "this isn't for everyone"

The good news: honesty is a *differentiator* in this feed. Every competitor ad screams. A calm one interrupts differently.

## PART 3 — Shoot direction

### 3.1 Setup (do this once, all three scripts use it)

| | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Camera** | Phone, rear camera, 4K if available, 30fps. Locked on a tripod — no handheld, no zoom, no movement. |
| **Orientation** | **Shoot vertical 9:16.** We crop to 4:5 and 1:1 in the edit. Never the reverse. |
| **Framing** | Mid-shot: waist up. Eyes on the **upper third line**. Leave headroom for the headline band. |
| **Eyeline** | Directly into the lens. Not at the screen. Tape a small arrow above the lens if it helps. |
| **Distance** | Roughly arm's length plus a step — close enough to feel like conversation, not an announcement. |
| **Audio** | Collar mic, non-negotiable. Phone mic will kill this. Record in a carpeted or soft-furnished room; no fan, no AC hum, close the window. |
| **Lighting** | Face a window, daylight, no direct sun. Or one soft light at 45°. Never overhead-only — it shadows the eyes. |
| **Background** | Clean, uncluttered, slightly out of focus. A plain wall works. Avoid: bed, kitchen, busy bookshelf, anything with a visible brand. |
| **Wardrobe** | Solid-colour shirt in navy `#0A2A43` or white. **No stripes, no checks, no small patterns** — they moiré on phone sensors. Collar, no tie. |

### 3.2 Performance direction, beat by beat

- **First 3 seconds:** No "Hi", no "Namaste", no "Main Gagan hoon". The first word is the hook. Introduce yourself at second 30 or not at all.
- **Hands:** Let them into frame. Lamba's best-performing frame has him mid-gesture. Stillness reads as stiffness.
- **Pace:** Hook fast, teaching beat slow, CTA calm. The teaching beat is where trust is earned — it's the only part he should slow down for.
- **The pauses:** Where the script breaks a line, actually stop. Those are edit points; the editor needs them.
- **Energy:** Convinced, not excited. He knows something useful and is telling you plainly. If it sounds like a sales pitch, do another take.
- **Retakes:** 3 per script. Keep take 3. Don't chase perfection — a small stumble reads as human and often outperforms a clean read.

**Rules from BRAND.md that the editor must not break:**
- Cyan `#4AD0FF` is accent only — a thin underline or a small shape. **Never cyan text on light, never white text on cyan.** Both fail contrast.
- One blue CTA per frame. Not two.
- Light-dominant. 60% canvas/white. Never a wall of blue.
- Logo on a quiet field only — white, pale blue `#E8F2FB`, or navy. Never over the video.
- No black drop shadows, no glows, no gradients.

**Headline variants** (one per script, so the frame itself is A/B tested too):

- Script A → **"Losing Years in Pharma Without Realising It?"**
- Script B → **"Can You Answer This MedTech Interview Question?"**
- Script C → **"'Good Candidate. Doesn't Understand the Industry.'"**

### 3.4 Edit spec

1. **Cut the first breath.** Whatever the take starts with, trim to the first consonant of the hook. Zero dead air.
2. **Burned-in captions, always.** ~85% of this feed watches on mute. Jakarta 600, white, subtle dark scrim, 2–4 words per card, synced tight. This is the single highest-ROI thing in the edit.
3. **Punch-in at the turn.** One 10% scale-up at the "teaching beat" (Script A: 0:12, B: 0:14, C: 0:16). One only. It re-grabs attention right where drop-off happens.
4. **Music:** none or barely-there ambient at ~10%. Lamba's ads have effectively no music bed and they're working. Voice carries it.
5. **Text overlays:** at most two per video, on the key nouns — `Class A–D` and `Foundation Module`. Fraunces, navy. Do not decorate.
6. **End card:** 2 seconds. Logo on pale blue `#E8F2FB`, one line — "Free Masterclass · Register" — one blue button. Nothing else.
7. **Exports per script:** 9:16 (Reels/Stories), 4:5 (feed — the volume driver), 1:1 (backup). Same master, three crops. Keep faces and captions inside safe areas on all three.

## PART 4 — Claim check (do not skip)

Every claim in these scripts was checked against `BRAND.md` §7 (content integrity — ASCI / Consumer Protection Act 2019 exposure).

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Foundation Module content — device map, Class A–D, India manufacturing/PLI, import ecosystem, high-value roles | ✅ Verbatim from your live curriculum (Module F) |
| "Zero MedTech experience required" | ✅ Matches your published FAQ |
| "Yeh job guarantee nahi hai" | ✅ Actively reinforces your no-guarantee policy — an asset, not a risk |
| "MedTech market expand ho raha hai" | ⚠️ Directionally true but unsourced. Keep it qualitative as written. **Do not add a number** unless you can cite it on the landing page. |
| **"Ninety percent pharma professionals ko nahi pata"** (Script B) | 🔴 **FABRICATED — must change before shooting.** Replace with: *"Bahut logon ko nahi pata."* Loses nothing, removes the liability entirely. |
| Script A "Ek hafte mein" | ⚠️ Only say this if Foundation is genuinely a one-week module. Otherwise cut the phrase — the line works without it. |
| Scarcity ("Limited") | ✅ Only if the cohort cap is real. If there's no cap, remove it. |

**Voice check vs BRAND.md §6** — banned words (10x, guru, secret, hack, life-changing, guaranteed, hustle) do not appear. Script A and C both use the "this isn't for everyone" honesty lever the brand spec explicitly prefers over a guarantee. Student is the hero in all three; Gagan is the guide.

**Funnel check vs §8** — all three CTAs go to the free masterclass, not a cold paid page. Correct per spec.

## Suggested test plan

Run all three at equal budget for 5 days. Watch **3-second hold rate** first, not CTR — that tells you which hook works. Then judge cost per registration.

Prediction, for what it's worth: **Script A** wins on volume (broadest pain), **Script B** wins on cost per registration (the question format creates a real curiosity gap and self-selects for people who feel the gap). Script C is your retargeting asset — it's the most credible and the least interruptive, which is exactly what you want on a second touch.

Whichever wins, keep the frame and swap only the headline for the next round. That's Lamba's actual operating system: three ideas, endlessly refreshed, never rebuilt.
