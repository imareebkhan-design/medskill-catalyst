Use this exact copy:

**ROLE 01 — Medical Device Sales / Territory Manager** · Low · No engineering degree
- What it is: The company's face in hospitals — selling medical devices to surgeons, doctors and procurement teams across your assigned territory.
- Day-to-day: Visiting hospitals and operating theatres, demoing devices and supporting live cases, building doctor and KOL relationships, negotiating with procurement, and tracking your territory's pipeline against targets. Relationship-driven, field-heavy work.

**ROLE 02 — Clinical Research / Trials Coordinator** · ★ Very low · No engineering degree
- What it is: Running and monitoring device clinical trials so they stay compliant, safe and on-protocol.
- Day-to-day: Site initiation and monitoring visits, reviewing case report forms and source data, ensuring GCP and protocol compliance, coordinating with investigators and sites, and tracking patient safety and trial documentation.

**ROLE 03 — Regulatory Affairs Associate** · ★ Low · No engineering degree
- What it is: Getting devices approved and keeping them compliant with CDSCO, MDR and other regulators.
- Day-to-day: Preparing and compiling technical files and submission dossiers, checking documents for conformity, tracking regulatory changes, liaising with authorities on submissions, and supporting post-market and adverse-event reporting.

**ROLE 04 — Clinical Application Specialist** · Moderate · No engineering mandated · tech/clinical preferred
- What it is: The clinical expert who trains doctors and staff to use a device correctly and safely.
- Day-to-day: Training clinicians and end-users on device use, supporting procedures in the clinical environment, troubleshooting issues, ensuring safe and optimal use, and feeding user insight back to sales and product teams.

**ROLE 05 — Market Access / Health Economics (HEOR)** · High · No engineering degree
- What it is: Proving a device's economic value so payers and hospitals will pay for it.
- Day-to-day: Building cost-effectiveness and budget-impact models, running literature reviews, writing reimbursement/HTA dossiers, analysing value versus alternatives, and turning clinical data into the economic case for payers.

**ROLE 06 — Quality / Design Assurance Engineer** · High · Engineering usually required
- What it is: The guardian of product quality and design controls across the device lifecycle.
- Day-to-day: Applying design and change controls, running risk assessments and FMEA, leading CAPA and root-cause analysis, conducting audits against ISO 13485, and verifying products meet safety and performance specs.

**ROLE 07 — R&D / Product Development Engineer** · Very high · Engineering degree required
- What it is: The engineer who designs and builds the device itself, from concept to launch.
- Day-to-day: Designing and prototyping devices, running design verification and validation testing, documenting design history and specs, solving technical problems, and working with quality, regulatory and manufacturing to reach launch.

**Rules:** one idea per slide, ≤ ~45 words in the day-to-day box, keep type large and mobile-legible, do not crowd the slide. Cover (slide 1) and CTA (slide 9) stay unchanged.
