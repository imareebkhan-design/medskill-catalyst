If your strength is talking to clinicians, there's a MedTech role built entirely around it. Most pharma people have never heard its name.

You voted clinical conversation as a skill that's already yours. This is the role it becomes.

Clinical application specialist.

What it actually is

You're the clinical expert behind a device. You train doctors and nurses to use it, support them through procedures, and become the person a hospital calls to get the most out of the equipment. Part trainer, part clinical support, part the person they trust in the room.

Who hires for it

Imaging and diagnostics companies like Siemens Healthineers, GE HealthCare and Philips. Surgical and interventional device makers. Most large equipment manufacturers. If you have a clinical or strong science background and you're good with people, you're already most of the way there.

The day to day

Time in hospitals and labs. Running training. Troubleshooting. Sitting in on procedures. Less about closing a sale, more about making sure the device delivers once it's bought. For a lot of pharma and life-sciences people, this feels closer to why they studied science in the first place.

Honest difficulty: medium

And friendlier to fresh and early-career people than sales is. Your degree and clinical understanding carry real weight, and the company trains you on the specific device.

The salary question

There's a genuine range, and it rewards specialisation over time. The real figures go up on screen in the masterclass, not in a caption.
