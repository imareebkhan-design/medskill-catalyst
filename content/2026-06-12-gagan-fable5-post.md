## POST TEXT (humanized — copy below this line)

Two days ago an AI ran a drug discovery project with no human help.

14 protein targets. 9 came out with strong drug candidates. It handled the whole thing end to end, the way a trained scientist would, and it matched or beat experienced human operators. Anthropic published this two days back along with their new model, Claude Fable 5.

I will be honest, my first reaction was discomfort. Work that takes our industry months at the bench, finished by a model while the team slept.

Since the news broke I have heard two reactions. Either "AI is coming for pharma jobs" or "these demos never reach a real lab." I think both are wrong, and I will tell you why.

The model did not replace the scientist. It replaced the waiting. Those long months of iteration between an idea and a candidate molecule. What it cannot do is decide which disease is worth chasing, or whether a molecule makes sense for Indian patients and will survive our regulatory path. That judgment still needs someone trained in medicine.

Now the part that matters for you. Anthropic is opening this capability to vetted biomedical companies. So over the next year or so, pharma and medtech companies will start hiring people who can sit between the science and these systems. People who can question what the machine proposes instead of blindly accepting it.

A software engineer cannot do that job. He cannot tell you if a candidate makes clinical sense.

A pharmacist can. So can a doctor, or a life sciences graduate, with one new layer of skill on top of the training they already have.

The threat to your career is not the AI. It is waiting for things to settle while others learn to work with it. Things are not going to settle.

Tell me honestly in the comments, have you used any AI tool in your actual work yet? Not for timepass. For real work. I read every reply.
